05 — Develop, renovate, convert

Property development

A property project is financed in stages: acquiring the land or the building, obtaining consents, carrying out the works, reaching practical completion, then selling or letting. Each phase has its own timetable, budget and uncertainties. We help developers, property companies and professional owners present the transaction as a whole, with an identified exit, and source financing that genuinely tracks how the project unfolds.

01

The projects we can support

A project's financing is built around its phases, its total cost and how it will be exited.

  • Financing the construction of a new building, from the land to practical completion, then its sale or letting.
  • Undertaking the heavy refurbishment of an asset you hold, whether or not it remains partly in operation during the works.
  • Changing the use of an office, industrial or hotel building, once the consents have been obtained.
  • Acquiring land or a building to be redeveloped, before the planning permission is beyond challenge.
  • Completing the financing of a project already under way, when the budget or the timetable has changed.
  • Replacing the construction facility, once the building is completed and let, with long-term debt.

02

How we approach a project

We start from the timetable. A project unfolds in distinct phases: control of the land, obtaining consents and seeing out the challenge period, tendering the works, construction, practical completion, then sales or letting. Each phase governs the next, and each can run late. We check with you that the timetable presented is realistic. We identify the milestones that release drawdowns and the points where a slippage would weigh on the cost of the transaction and on its exit.

Next comes the budget as a whole: the price of the land or the building, the cost of the works, professional fees, financing costs, taxes and the contingency. A lender does not finance a construction cost but a complete transaction, together with what the sponsor commits to it. We make sure that the planned equity, whether cash or an asset contribution, is consistent with the risk of each phase. We then set out the margins and exit assumptions on which the financing plan rests.

Finally, the exit determines the structure. A block sale, individual unit sales or letting followed by a refinancing: each exit calls for a different repayment profile, different security and different lenders. We build the file around the chosen exit and its fallback routes, then present it to the counterparts suited to the type of project: banks, top-up lenders or private debt funds. We then compare the terms offered with you, in coordination with your advisers.

03

What the review relies on

A project is assessed on precise documents; for a first conversation, a description and rough figures are enough.

  1. 01Presentation of the project: location, scheme, floor areas and intended use of the units.
  2. 02Status of consents: planning permission applied for, granted or beyond challenge, and any appeals.
  3. 03Development appraisal, with the breakdown of costs and the contingency.
  4. 04Timetable by phase, from control of the land to practical completion.
  5. 05Project vehicle, equity committed or available, and its origin.
  6. 06Intended exit: block sale, individual unit sales or letting, with its assumptions.
  7. 07Track record of the sponsor and the team assembled: architect or lead consultant, contractors, sales agent.

04

Our role, and its limits

We act neither as developer, nor as architect or lead consultant, nor as sales agent. We do not design the project, do not supervise the site and do not sell the units. Our role is confined to the financing: analysing the transaction, preparing the file, sourcing lenders and comparing terms. Legal, tax, technical and planning questions belong to your advisers, with whom we work.

A level of equity, a loan-to-cost ratio, a rate or a term can only be set once the project is reviewed: they depend on the status of consents, the progress of the works, the chosen exit and the lenders consulted. We do not promise financing will be obtained, and a project may remain unfinanced if its timetable or its budget is not convincing. We commit to rigour in the analysis and candour in our feedback, even when it is negative.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

01At what stage should financing be sought?

A first conversation can take place early, as soon as the project can be described. A formal request, however, requires settled elements: land under control or under option, a planning application at least lodged, a detailed budget and a timetable by phase. Before that, a lender will only give an indication of interest. Preparing the file in advance makes it possible to approach the right counterparts at the moment the project genuinely becomes financeable.

02Does the planning permission need to be beyond challenge?

Not always, but it changes how the transaction is read. As long as a challenge remains possible, the lender is financing an uncertainty: the funding available is scarcer and more expensive. Some lenders are willing to step in before the challenge period has expired, on the land for instance, before the construction facility takes over. The status of the permission therefore determines which phase can be financed, and with whom.

03How is the construction financed?

Most often in tranches, in step with progress on site. The lender releases funds against the contractors' applications for payment, certified by the lead consultant or an independent monitor, once the equity has been committed. This mechanism requires close monitoring and cash resources able to absorb the gap between expenditure and drawdowns. The budget and timetable presented at the outset serve as the reference throughout the works.

04What happens if the timetable slips?

A delay extends the period during which the debt runs without income and may bring the loan's maturity closer to the exit date. It is better to inform the lender early, with a revised timetable and an updated budget. Depending on the case, the solution may be an extension, additional equity or a top-up facility. A timetable built with margins from the outset limits how often these situations arise.
