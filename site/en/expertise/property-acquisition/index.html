04 — Assets held through a company or investment vehicle

Investment and commercial property acquisition

Acquiring a property asset for business or investment purposes commits a company, its balance sheet and a timetable. We source and structure, on the borrower's side, the financing of that acquisition, whether it concerns a single asset or a portfolio. The file must give a clear picture of the asset, its income, the structure acquiring it and how the debt will be carried and then repaid.

01

The acquisitions we review

Within the scope of the services we offer, the acquisition concerns a residential investment, commercial or hotel asset, or a portfolio of assets. It is always held through a company or an investment vehicle.

  • Acquiring a residential building intended for letting, held through a dedicated company or a holding company.
  • Financing commercial premises, whether let to third parties or occupied by your own business.
  • Acquiring the premises of a hotel through a dedicated company.
  • Acquiring in a single transaction a portfolio of assets sold by the same vendor.
  • Financing the purchase of shares in a property company rather than of the asset itself.
  • Combining the acquisition with a programme of works before the asset is let or re-let.

02

How we read an acquisition

We start with the asset and with what it earns. Acquisition financing runs for years: its soundness rests on the asset's capacity to service the debt, year after year, in the condition in which it is bought. We therefore review the leases, the quality and diversity of the tenants, the costs that remain with the owner, the foreseeable works and the letting position at completion. Where the asset is occupied by your own business, it is that business we analyse. This reading shows the level of debt the asset can carry, before any discussion of terms.

We then examine the structure making the acquisition and how it will be financed. A dedicated company, a holding company, a vehicle with several shareholders: each configuration alters the available security, the undertakings asked of the shareholders and how a lender will read the file. With your advisers, we prepare the balance between equity and debt and a repayment schedule matched to the holding period and to the exit intended when it ends. The security proposed remains consistent with the asset. For a portfolio, the question of a single facility or asset-by-asset financing arises early, as it shapes future disposals.

An acquisition's timetable is set by the vendor, the sale agreement and its conditions precedent. We fit the search for financing to that timetable and flag early what could strain it: a valuation the lender will require, a technical survey, documentation to gather. The file presented to lenders includes a description of the asset, its tenancy schedule, the borrowing structure, the funding plan and the intended repayment schedule. We submit it to the lenders whose policy matches the asset, lead the discussions and help you weigh the offers received through to completion, with your solicitor or notary and your advisers.

03

What the review requires

A first conversation can be held on the asset, the price and the timetable; the review itself relies on the following information.

  1. 01Description of the asset, its location, its condition and the planned works.
  2. 02Tenancy schedule: current leases, rents, expiry dates, service charges and rental deposits or guarantees.
  3. 03Purchase price, associated costs and any valuation material available.
  4. 04Intended acquisition structure, shareholders and source of the equity.
  5. 05Timetable of the transaction: sale agreement, conditions precedent, expected completion date.
  6. 06Financial position of the borrower, its shareholders and related companies.
  7. 07Holding period and strategy for the asset: retention, works, disposal.

04

The limits of our role

The level of debt an asset can carry, the term and the conditions cannot be read off the purchase price. They emerge from the review of the income, the structure and the security, and then from the responses of the lenders consulted. Our role is confined to assets held through a company or an investment vehicle and to the scope of the services we offer, and we would rather say so early when a transaction falls outside it.

We take no view on the merits of the acquisition itself. The price, the asset's quality and the holding strategy are matters for you and your advisers, as are the legal and tax aspects of the chosen structure. We do not lend, do not source assets on your behalf and cannot assure you that a lender will take on the transaction. What we owe you is a rigorous analysis and a file lenders can assess on a clear basis.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

01Which types of property asset do you review?

We review the financing of assets held through a company or an investment vehicle, within the scope of the services we offer. These include residential investment buildings, commercial premises let to third parties or occupied by your own business, hotel premises and portfolios sold by a single vendor. The purchase of shares in a property company also falls within this field. What matters to a lender is less the category of the asset than its income, its condition and the structure acquiring it.

02Do I need a company to borrow?

Our role concerns assets held through a company, a holding company or an investment vehicle. The form of that structure is not a neutral choice: it determines the security available, the undertakings asked of the shareholders and how the lender will read the file. Choosing it, together with its legal and tax aspects, is a matter for you and your advisers. We then take it into account when structuring the financing.

03When should I consult a financing adviser?

As early as possible, ideally before the sale agreement is signed. That is when the timetable, the conditions precedent and the balance between equity and debt are decided. An adviser consulted early can flag what will put the financing under strain and prepare the file while the other aspects of the transaction are being negotiated. Consulted late, they can only work within a timetable already fixed.

04What should I gather for a first conversation?

A first conversation can be held on the description of the asset, the price, the intended structure and the timetable. The tenancy schedule and any valuation material are helpful if they already exist. The review itself will then require the accounts, the source of the equity, the shareholders' position and the holding strategy. Not everything needs to be in hand before you present the transaction to us.
